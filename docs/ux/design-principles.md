# EV Compare — UX Design Principles

These principles apply to all UX design, wireframing, visual design and frontend implementation work.

## 1. Show, don't explain the interface

The interface should communicate how to use it primarily through its structure, affordances and behavior.

Avoid instructional text for self-evident interactions.

If an interaction requires extensive explanation, first reconsider the interaction itself.

## 2. No unnecessary didactic copy

Do not add explanatory text merely to demonstrate that the product is accessible, flexible or beginner-friendly.

Avoid statements describing what users are *not* required to do, such as "No questionnaire required".

Use contextual explanations when they provide genuinely useful information, especially about EV concepts, calculations, assumptions or limitations.

Do not confuse product education with explaining the interface.

## 3. Natural, concise language

Prefer direct sentences, short headings and clear information hierarchy.

Avoid unnecessary subordinate clauses, repetitive descriptions, promotional filler and artificial reassurance.

Do not combine several independent messages into one long heading.

For example, prefer:

**Trova l'auto elettrica adatta a te.**

*E capisci perché.*

over a single compound sentence.

## 4. Purposeful progressive disclosure

Do not attempt to represent every Product Vision principle on every screen.

Include functionality where it naturally serves the user's current task.

Advanced features and explanations should be discoverable without dominating the primary experience.

## 5. Respect user autonomy

Do not design patronizing interfaces.

Do not introduce mandatory guidance, educational steps or unnecessary confirmation prompts.

Support users with different levels of expertise without classifying them into rigid beginner/expert modes.

## 6. Visual clarity over feature density

Do not overload screens with controls, labels, explanations or indicators simply because they are available.

Prioritize the information and actions relevant to the current context.

Progressive disclosure should reduce unnecessary complexity, not hide essential functionality.

## 7. Critical design judgment

Treat the Product Vision as a set of goals and constraints, not as a checklist that must be visibly demonstrated in every wireframe.

Make independent design decisions and explain meaningful trade-offs in the accompanying design review rather than adding explanatory copy to the interface itself.

When reviewing a design, explicitly look for unnecessary microcopy, artificial interactions and features included only to satisfy a requirement superficially.
